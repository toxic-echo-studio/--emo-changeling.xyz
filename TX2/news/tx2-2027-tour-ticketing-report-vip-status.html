Race Against the Clock or Ticketing Smoke & Mirrors? What’s Actually Disappearing Ahead of TX2’s 2027 Tour

Author: Mighn Taghor | Published: 2026-11-22

  • Shows really are shrinking, but don’t panic: the TX2 camp is flashing low-ticket warnings, yet a “sold out” tag at one vendor often doesn’t mean the doors are locked at the official box office.
  • Prague plot twists (and across the rest of Europe): Czechia’s Rock Café has already burned through 50% of its tickets without sounding any loud alarms, and the tour map is packed with venue curveballs.
  • EU VIP passes are spin the bottle: Sound Rink is handling the packages, but with zero public counters out there, dragging your feet means risking a total L.

Not All Ticket Vendors Are Built the Same (Where It’s Actually Getting Tight)

The latest low ticket warnings coming out of Evan Thomas’s camp for most of the UK/EU run aren’t just empty TikTok hype - still, it pays to check the actual box offices before throwing extra cash at scalpers.

The UK is feeling the squeeze the hardest. In Nottingham (March 12), the promoter already slapped on a “Last few tickets” badge, Glasgow (March 10) is flashing “Limited Availability” on Ticketmaster, and London (March 4) completely wiped out its wheelchair allocation. Nothing sums up how ticketing works better than Manchester (March 9), though: Gigantic is already down to a waiting list and the venue’s own access area is gone, yet Ticketmaster UK still has tickets sitting there under “Limited Availability”.

Here in Warsaw (Feb 20, Hydrozagadka), it’s the exact same story: Tier 1 and family passes at Knock Out are Sold Out, while Tier 2 is still floating around. The takeaway? When one outlet (or resale sharks like Viagogo/StubHub with their “Almost sold out” scare banners) hits zero, the official promoter often still has regular-priced tickets waiting. (For some US context: January’s ShipRocked 2027 cruise sold out every single cabin, and May’s Welcome To Rockville already burned through its RV camping spots, even though festival passes themselves are still up for grabs.)

Prague Plot Twists and Good Ol’ Tour Map Chaos

Against all that noise, Prague (Feb 21, Rock Café) is worth a closer look. Nobody’s hitting the red panic button yet, but Czech promoter RFP Concerts put an honest counter right on the page: 50% of tickets are already gone. Half the room is spoken for before half the crew even thought about making the cross-border trip.

The rest of the mainland map brings plenty of classic punk-rock disorder too: Tampere’s Olympia shut its doors and left fans hanging on refunds, Milan’s ticketing sites can’t agree on whether it’s Santeria Toscana 31 or Lime Club, and Switzerland’s PETZI has Solothurn marked as postponed - even while local box offices keep spitting out tickets.

EU VIP Passes: Sound Rink and Spin the Bottle With Fate

If you’re aiming for more than just throwing elbows in the pit (like actually meeting the band and handing over that Warsaw pigeon poster), Sound Rink (eu.soundrink.com) is running the EU/UK VIP packages, just like the band confirmed back in June. Bailey Spinn threw her own separate VIP Upgrade into the mix there too.

The catch? TX2’s own VIPs are sitting in a total info black hole - there are zero public counters online showing how many spots are left. With regular tickets, you can still shop around between official box offices, but if you wait around on a VIP pass, you’re playing spin the bottle with fate.
