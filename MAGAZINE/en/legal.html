Press Law Provisions

§ X. Rectifications (Corrections)

Any person concerned by published information is entitled to submit a formal rectification of inaccurate or untrue statements contained in materials published on this website or across the magazine's video channels.

A rectification request must be submitted in writing within 21 days from the date of publication, must be factual, and must pertain strictly to verifiable facts.

The editorial board publishes rectifications free of charge, at the earliest possible date corresponding to the original format of publication (website publication or video feature).

The editorial board may refuse publication exclusively under circumstances provided by statutory law, particularly where the rectification fails formal requirements, is non-factual, or breaches applicable law.

In the event of refusal, the applicant may seek publication of the rectification through judicial proceedings before the competent court.

§ X.1. Journalistic Privilege & Confidentiality

The editorial team and collaborating journalists safeguard information identifying confidential sources, as well as all information entrusted under professional confidence.

Disclosure of journalistic secrets is permissible solely under specific statutory exemptions and pursuant to a legally binding court order.

§ X.2. Prohibition of Suppressing Press Criticism

Any actions aimed at suppressing or curtailing legitimate press criticism—including exertion of pressure, threats, refusal of public information, or retaliatory measures—are strictly unlawful.

Press criticism is conducted diligently and in accordance with the standards of professional journalistic ethics.

§ X.3. Obstruction of Journalistic Work

Obstructing the execution of journalistic duties—particularly through withholding public records, exerting unlawful pressure, intimidation, or impeding the gathering of editorial materials—is prohibited.

Measures infringing upon the freedom of journalistic activity constitute statutory violations under the Polish Press Law Act.

Notice to U.S. Counsel & Foreign Entities

(A Gentle Reminder Regarding Jurisdictional Realities)

Dear Counsel,

If you are accessing this publication with the intention of serving a Notice, Cease & Desist Demand, Litigation Threat, DMCA Takedown Request, or any comparable instrument on behalf of a United States client, we invite your careful review of several threshold procedural realities. Experience demonstrates that many such demands are dispatched with admirable zeal, yet a discernible unfamiliarity with the governing law.

a. Jurisdiction & Governing Law

This publication operates strictly under the legal sovereignty of the Republic of Poland and the European Union.

  • Applicable Substantive Law: The Polish Act of 26 January 1984 – Press Law (Journal of Laws 1984 No. 5, item 24, as amended) and the Polish Act on Copyright and Related Rights (Journal of Laws 1994 No. 24, item 83).
  • Jurisdiction: Exclusive territorial and subject-matter jurisdiction lies with the courts of the Republic of Poland.
  • Foreign Orders: United States subpoenas, court orders, or extrajudicial demands carry no direct extraterritorial legal effect within this jurisdiction unless formally recognized through exequatur proceedings under Article 1145 et seq. of the Polish Code of Civil Procedure (KPC).

We appreciate that this may diverge from assumptions cultivated by domestic litigation practices in the United States.

b. Press Status & Protected Editorial Activity

This publication is a registered press outlet subject to formal editorial oversight.

  • Content labeled [OPINION], [ESSAY], or [SATIRE] constitutes protected value judgments under Article 10 of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR) and established jurisprudence of the European Court of Human Rights.
  • Journalistic Confidentiality: Sources and informants are strictly protected under Article 15 of the Polish Press Law Act.

Efforts to compel source disclosure should accordingly be directed elsewhere.

c. Statutory Rectification Procedure

Alleged factual inaccuracies must be addressed exclusively through the statutory rectification procedure established under Article 31a et seq. of the Polish Press Law Act.

  • Informal emails, automated form letters, or demands submitted outside the strict 21-day statutory window carry no legal effect.
  • Counsel may wish to note that assertions of American-style "urgency" do not abbreviate or circumvent statutory limitation periods under Polish law.

d. Unfounded Claims & Anti-SLAPP Countermeasures

Unfounded or bad-faith takedown demands directed at hosting providers or CDN edge services—particularly those premised on U.S. DMCA provisions that do not apply within the European Union—may trigger:

  • Counterclaims for tortious damages under Article 415 of the Polish Civil Code,
  • Criminal referrals under Article 44 of the Polish Press Law Act (unlawful suppression of press criticism),
  • Immediate summary dismissal and cost-shifting sanctions under EU Anti-SLAPP frameworks.

We trust this encourages a moment of measured reflection before transmitting automated notifications.

e. Service of Process

Formal service of process must be effected in writing, accompanied by a certified Polish translation executed by a sworn translator (tłumacz przysięgły), and authenticated with an Apostille pursuant to the Hague Convention.

  • Designated Legal Inquiries: legal@toxic-echo.studio

f. Legal Disclaimer

This informational notice does not constitute legal advice. It outlines the procedural boundaries within which this publication operates—a courtesy extended to prevent unnecessary administrative theater.
