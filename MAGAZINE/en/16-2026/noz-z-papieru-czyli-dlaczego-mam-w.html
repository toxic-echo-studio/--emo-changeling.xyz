A Knife Made of Paper: Why I Don't Give a Damn About Alt-Account Therapists

Another brutal summer heatwave, zero sleep, and a fresh plague of armchair psychologists lurking behind blank profiles, convinced they've mapped out your entire psyche from a 15-second teaser clip. This vlog was originally meant to tackle a heavy, complex subject, but stress and a sudden shutdown had other plans. Instead of spiraling into a total crying meltdown, it turned into raw, unfiltered venting about self-proclaimed web geniuses who have never skimmed an actual scientific paper, yet feel fully entitled to lecture you on mental health. Their instant diagnosis? You’re allegedly "self-sabotaging," you "aren't trying," and you just secretly enjoy being miserable.

It’s textbook victim-blaming from people who don't know the first thing about you. You mention living with neurodivergence, battling mental health symptoms, or surviving a brutal past, and the comments immediately twist it into your own fault—claiming you got bullied in school because you refused to obey the social food chain and chose to be cringe but free. None of these anonymous experts witnessed the grueling years of therapy, firing incompetent shrinks, or breaking painful trauma bonds with toxic family environments. They never saw the agony of unlearning the fawn response—that sickening survival reflex where your reptile brain forces you to people-please your abuser on autopilot while your actual consciousness watches in disgust.

Sitting in front of a lens, owning an alternative look, openly talking about mental struggles, and experimenting with crossdressing or wearing a skirt isn’t a sign of being broken. It’s a massive win against years of conditioning and brainwashing. Talking openly about your weak spots strips haters of all their ammunition; you’re handing them a weapon, but the blade is made of f***ing paper. Don't let random online trolls or toxic relatives gaslight you into thinking your progress isn't real or that getting help messed you up. Hit block, ditch the noise, and remember that nobody knows your battles better than you do.
